1. Model explanation

The goal is a regression model that predicts selling_price from a car’s features. Two models are available in step 3; each learns a weight for every feature by fitting the training data.

Linear regression

Fits the best straight-line (hyperplane) relationship: ŷ = b + w₁x₁ + w₂x₂ + …. The weights minimise the squared error. There are no attributes to set. A coefficient shows how much the prediction moves when that feature rises by one unit (one standard deviation if you standardized).

Polynomial regression

Adds squared and cubed terms and interactions (such as year·max_power), then fits a linear model on them, so it can follow curves. Degree sets how flexible the curve is: higher degrees fit the training data better but can overfit. In Model development, choose a start and end degree to train every degree in that inclusive range, then compare their train and test scores in Model evaluation.

How the models are scored

R² is the share of the variation in price explained relative to predicting its average. Explained variance is 1 minus the variance of residual errors divided by the target variance; it can differ from R² when errors have a nonzero mean. MSE is the average squared error, and RMSE is its square root, in the target’s units (log units if you log-transformed price). For all four metrics, use the test set to compare generalization; higher R² and explained variance, and lower MSE and RMSE, are better.
